# Implementation post-mortem

Write a short HTML page for the person after implementation ends. Lead with the outcome and whether the original feature actually works; keep the approved plan as the historical baseline rather than rewriting it to match the result.

- **Outcome:** completed, blocked, or failed; delivered behavior, unfinished requirements, tested revision, and links to the plan, PR, verification evidence, and conversation viewer.
- **How implementation went:** brief account by package, including what was reused or created and what worked well.
- **Where it got stuck:** concrete blockers, unsuccessful attempts, root cause when known, how it recovered, or what remains unresolved. Link relevant trace moments; distinguish observed facts from hypotheses.
- **Surprises and deviations:** original approach → actual approach, why it changed, impact on scope/schema/architecture, and user approval where required. Say explicitly when there were no material deviations.
- **Verification and review:** results against “How we will know it works,” review mode and findings/fixes, remaining gaps. Do not call untested behavior complete.
- **Lessons:** a few actionable improvements for future plans or runs, based on this evidence. Include time/token/cost figures only when measured and scoped; mark estimates and missing telemetry.

Link back to the hub and approved cover sheet. Keep it factual, concise, and useful to someone who did not watch the run; do not paste raw tool logs or invent reasons for delays.
